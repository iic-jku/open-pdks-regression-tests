Extraction bench · IHP SG13G2

Three extractors, one deck, six disagreements

Thirty-one dummy metal layouts with hand-computable parasitics, put through Magic, kpex's 2.5D engine and a mesh-converged FasterCap solve. The capacitance and resistance models are exact. Six things around them are not, and two of the template's make targets disagree with each other by 16 % on the same layout.

Verdict

Exact

Capacitance to substrate, sheet resistance, contact resistance. All three implementations reproduce the deck to six digits on all seven metals and all four via types, including the 1/n scaling for parallel cuts.

Corroborated

The deck's substrate coefficients are consistent with a field solve on Metal1 to Metal4, within the roughly 10 % the triangulation was still moving. Sound, not yet precise.

Defect 1

EXT_MODE=3 double-counts coupling at MINDELAY=0. Total extracted capacitance on the template inverter goes from 248 fF to 511 fF.

Defect 2

EXT_MODE=3 collapses resistor networks at the shipped MINDELAY=1. A four-terminal net loses two arms and one port is left floating.

Defect 3

EXT_MODE=1 discards interconnect coupling rather than lumping it to ground. The inverter input nets come out with zero wiring capacitance.

Defect 4

magic-pex and klayout-pex are the same engine and still disagree. sak-pex.sh issues ext2spice lvs, which loses 40.5 fF of substrate capacitance on the inverter.

Defect 5

Magic's lateral coupling is half the deck value and 23 % to 56 % below a converged field solve at every spacing. kpex's 2.5D engine, reading the same coefficients, emits the full value.

Defect 6

Magic's halo model sends shielded plate-to-plate coupling to the substrate. On TopMetal1 over Metal1 it under-reports the coupling by 30 % and assigns 0.8 fF to the substrate where the field solve puts 0.13 fF there and 0.38 fF leaving upward.

What was compared, and how

deck

The arithmetic of ihp-sg13g2-extract.tech variant (), done by hand. The reference the PDK specifies.

Magic

sak-pex.sh, that is make magic-pex. Magic's implementation of that deck.

kpex 2.5D

kpex --2.5D. A second, independent implementation, reading the identical coefficients from ihp-sg13g2_tech.pb.json.

FasterCap

kpex --fastercap. A boundary-element field solve on the same stack. No model, no coefficients.

Those four columns are the whole point of the exercise. The deck and Magic tell you whether the tool implements the model. kpex's 2.5D engine is the control: it reads the same numbers, so where it disagrees with Magic exactly one of them has a bug. FasterCap then arbitrates, because it knows nothing about either.

The one target that is not a fourth opinion is make klayout-pex. It passes --magic --magic_mode CC, so it drives Magic. Its coupling capacitors on the inverter are bit-identical to make magic-pex: 26.3603, 15.8261, 9.20502, 0.02526 fF and so on, every digit. The repository has two PEX targets and one capacitance engine.

kpex's process stack matches the PDK cross-section exactly, which is what makes the field solve admissible as an arbiter: Metal1 z=1.04 t=0.42, Metal2 to Metal5 t=0.49, TopMetal1 t=2.0, TopMetal2 t=3.0, ILD k=4.1, all identical to sg13g2_for_EM.xs.

The structures

Each layout isolates one term. Substrate capacitance pairs a 50 x 50 um plate (A/P = 12.5) with a 0.5 x 50 um wire (A/P = 0.25) on the same layer, 60 um apart: two shapes give two equations, so the area and perimeter coefficients separate instead of trading off. Overlap centres a 10 x 10 um upper plate inside a 30 x 30 um lower one, so the upper plate sees nothing but the lower plate and its own edge fringe. Resistance uses a 1 um wide wire with a port at each end, 99 um apart. 

C_substrate  = area x areacap  +  perimeter x perimc          # aF/um2, aF/um
C_overlap    = overlap_area x overlap  +  edge x sideoverlap
C_sidewall   = k x length / (separation + offset)              # from defaultsidewall
R            = squares x resist  +  cuts x contact             # mOhm/sq, mOhm/cut

Defect 1  ·  Full-RC counts every coupling capacitance twice

Defect

EXT_MODE=3 with MINDELAY=0

Two parallel Metal1 wires, 0.5 um wide, 200 um long, 0.2 um apart, each given a stub so both nets branch and qualify for resistance extraction. Same GDS, two settings: 

* EXT_MODE=2, the capacitance reference
C0 s1 s0        19.1567f      # coupling
C1 s1 sub       14.0930f
C2 s0 sub       14.0930f

* EXT_MODE=3, THRESHOLD=10000 MINRES=1000 MINDELAY=0
R0..R5          # correct, arm values within 0.1 %
C0 s1 s0        19.1567f      # coupling, still here
C1 s1  w#       15.00077f  \
C2 b1  w#        4.35815f   | sum over net s1 = 33.2497 fF
C3 a1  w#        4.37121f   |       = 14.0930 + 19.1567
C8 s1.n0 w#      9.51957f  /  the coupling, added to ground as well

The relation is exact. Each net's substrate capacitance in the full-RC netlist equals its true substrate capacitance plus the whole of its coupling capacitance, spread over the RC nodes, while the coupling element itself is still emitted. Node s0 carries 52.4 fF where the correct answer is 33.25 fF. On the smallest possible case, two coupled wires with no resistor network at all, it reads 7.85871 = 3.06954 + 4.78917 on each node, again to the last digit.

Impact on the template inverter Total extracted capacitance rises from 248.4 fF to 510.8 fF. The output node vout1 goes from 35.8 fF to 60.8 fF, an over-estimate of 70 %. A post-layout delay or power number taken from this netlist is pessimistic by roughly that much.

Defect 2  ·  The shipped MINDELAY collapses resistor networks

Defect

EXT_MODE=3 with the default MINDELAY=1

One Metal1 net shaped as a cross: four arms of 199.5, 199.5, 299.5 and 199 um meeting at one junction, a port at the end of each arm, nothing else in the layout.

R0 n.n0 e   32.9345          # 299.5 um at 110 mOhm/sq = 32.945
R1 n.n0 s   21.9565
R2 w n.n0   21.9565
R3 n.n0 n   21.8245

* THRESHOLD=10000 MINRES=1000 MINDELAY=1 -- the shipped defaults
.subckt cross_m1 w e s n
R0 n s      21.9565
R1 w n      21.9565

Two failures in one netlist. Port e is declared and connected to nothing, so it is a floating node in simulation. Port n survives but has been silently aliased onto the junction, so its 21.82 Ohm arm now reads zero. The same collapse happens on a three-arm tee, where a 32.93 Ohm arm disappears.

Any non-zero MINDELAY does this, and the value does not matter: 1, 2 and 10 behave alike. MINRES, the knob actually documented as the network simplification control, is innocent. It merges nothing at 1000 mOhm on 11 Ohm arms, exactly as it should.

Partial safety net The repo's scripts/check_pex_ports.py catches the fully dropped port and fails the target with exit 1. It cannot catch the aliased port, because that one is connected, just to the wrong node through zero ohms. The inverter's netlist passes the check while carrying 32 % fewer resistors than the same layout at MINDELAY=0 (1048 against 1539).
Defect

Two-terminal and unbranched nets get no resistance at all

A 1 um wide, 1000 um long Metal1 wire is 110 Ohm with 115 fF against the substrate, an RC delay of 12.6 ps. At the shipped defaults it extracts as an ideal short, and adding a third port in the middle changes nothing. Only THRESHOLD=0 together with MINDELAY=0 produces the resistor, and then it is right: R0 l r 109.891 against a hand value of 109.89.

The threshold behaves as documented on a branched net, where the network survives at THRESHOLD=10000 and disappears at 100000, bracketing the net's real 10.96 Ohm. On a straight net it drops the network at any non-zero threshold, including 1 mOhm. Magic's log states the split plainly: Nets extracted: 1 followed by Nets output: 0. The resistance is computed and then discarded.

What this hits in practice Point-to-point routes, a clock spine feeding one sink, supply stubs, any long wire between exactly two things. These are the nets whose resistance a designer most wants to see, and they are exactly the ones the default configuration omits.

Defect 3  ·  C-decoupled drops coupling instead of grounding it

Defect

EXT_MODE=1

Both macro READMEs and the sak-pex.sh header call mode 1 "C-decoupled", which a reader takes to mean coupling capacitors referred to ground. That is not what comes out. On the template inverter, mode 1 emits four capacitors in total: 

* EXT_MODE=1, the complete capacitor list
C0 vout1 VSS 41.61635f
C1 vout2 VSS 41.61635f
C2 vout3 VSS 41.61635f
C3 vout4 VSS 41.61635f
* VDD and all four vin nets carry no parasitic capacitance at all

In mode 2 the net vin1 carries 26.36 fF to VDD and 15.83 fF to vout1, so 42.2 fF of real wiring capacitance. In mode 1 it carries zero. The dummy bench shows the mechanism: two coupled wires come out with exactly the capacitance each would have in isolation, 4.8735 fF, instead of the 7.859 fF a properly decoupled extraction gives. Magic is not lumping the neighbour's coupling to ground, it is treating the neighbour as absent, which also removes the shielding of the substrate fringe.

Impact 33 % of the inverter's total capacitance, and 100 % of the wiring capacitance on a shielded signal net. Mode 1 is the fast option reached for at the chip top level, where routing is densest and the error is therefore largest.

Defect 4  ·  One engine, two targets, two answers

Defect

ext2spice lvs loses 16 % of the substrate capacitance

make klayout-pex and make magic-pex both run Magic, and their coupling capacitors are identical to the last digit. Their substrate capacitors are not. The only difference in how they drive Magic is one line: sak-pex.sh issues ext2spice lvs and kpex does not.

Every coupling capacitor is unchanged. All 40.5 fF of the difference is substrate capacitance, and four vin nets plus VDD come out with exactly zero.

The responsible sub-option is ext2spice hierarchy, which ext2spice lvs switches on. Setting it alone reproduces the loss exactly, and ext2spice lvs followed by ext2spice hierarchy off restores the full 288.94 fF. The other things the shortcut changes (global off, blackbox on, subcircuit top auto, format ngspice) each make no difference on their own.

It only bites on layouts containing devices. On the three device-less bench structures, hierarchy on and off give byte-identical netlists. On a single sg13g2_inv_1 the total is conserved at 0.71408 fF either way, but net A's 0.16306 fF of substrate capacitance is moved onto Y and VDD in exact halves. On the macro it is not conserved at all.

To remove any doubt about which of the two is right, the same standard cell was given a 0.5 x 200 um Metal2 wire on its gate net A, reached through one Via1 in the pin. That wire is worth about 15.8 fF of substrate capacitance by the deck, and there is nothing else it could belong to.

With hierarchy on, the wire's entire capacitance leaves the gate net and lands in two exact halves on the output and the supply. The total is conserved on this cell and not on the macro, so the failure is not even consistent about that, but in both cases the flat output is the one that puts the charge on the net that carries the metal.

Impact The repository ships two PEX targets that disagree by 16 % on the same layout, and the one most people run, magic-pex, is the one losing capacitance. Fixing it is one line: drop ext2spice lvs from sak-pex.sh and set the options it was wanted for explicitly.

Defect 5  ·  Lateral coupling is exactly half the deck value

The deck writes defaultsidewall allm1 metal1 28.735 -0.057. Magic emits 14.3675 x L / (s - 0.05), to five digits at every spacing over a 16-fold range: half the coefficient, with the offset quantised to whole lambda. Magic's tech reader halves the parsed value on the stated grounds that both edges of a pair get visited, and on this geometry the second visit does not happen.

kpex's 2.5D engine settles what that means. It reads the identical coefficients ("capacitance": 28.735, "offset": -0.057, "side_halo": 8 in ihp-sg13g2_tech.pb.json) and emits the full value: 10.0472 fF at 0.2 um, 1.93371 fF at 0.8 um, matching the deck formula to six digits at every spacing. Two implementations of one deck, differing by exactly two on this term and agreeing to six digits on every other term. One of them is wrong.

FasterCap at --delaunay_amax 0.1 --tolerance 0.01, matrix asymmetry 0.03 % to 0.16 % on every row, so these five values are converged. Mean absolute error against the field solve: Magic 44.9 %, kpex 2.5D 20.0 %.

So the evidence runs three ways and all three point the same direction. Magic and kpex 2.5D implement the same deck and agree everywhere except here, where they differ by exactly two. Magic applies a documented halving whose justification does not hold on this geometry. And the converged field solve sits above Magic at every one of five spacings, by 23 % to 56 %.

The remaining honest caveat is that the deck's own model is a poor fit at close spacing, 61 % high at 0.2 um. Fixing the factor of two would leave that. But 20 % mean error with the sign changing is a fit; 45 % mean error with the sign never changing is a bug.

Defect

The upper plate's edge charge goes to ground instead of to the plate below

Magic applies a halo fringe model that splits the upper plate's edge capacitance between the plate below and the substrate, capped by sidehalo 8. kpex 2.5D applies no such split. For adjacent layers the two land within 1.4 % of each other and it does not matter. For distant layers it matters a lot, and the field solve says the split is wrong: 

* TopMetal1 10x10 plate centred on a Metal1 30x30 plate
                        top-to-bottom   top-to-substrate   top-to-far-field
Magic                        1.6024             0.7957         (no such node)
kpex 2.5D                    2.3178             (none)         (no such node)
FasterCap                    2.2861             0.1267             0.3809

The upper plate is completely covered by the lower one, so almost nothing should reach the substrate, and the field solve puts 0.13 fF there. It also sends 0.38 fF upward into the far field, which Magic has no node for, so the fairest comparison gives Magic's substrate term 0.51 fF to account for. Magic assigns 0.80 fF, and takes it out of the plate-to-plate coupling, which it then under-reports by 30 %. The same shape appears on the other Metal1-based pair, Metal3 over Metal1, where Magic's coupling is 19 % below the solve and kpex 2.5D's 13 %. On adjacent layers the two models sit within 1.4 % of each other and the split is harmless. On TopMetal2 over TopMetal1 Magic is in fact the closer of the two, so this is a defect of the deep-stack case, not of the halo idea as such.

Why the direction matters This is not a magnitude error, it is charge sent to the wrong node. Capacitance to a signal net below and capacitance to ground behave completely differently: one carries crosstalk and Miller loading, the other does not. An extraction that moves 0.7 fF from the first to the second is optimistic about coupling in exactly the case, a wide plate over a distant plane, where a designer is relying on the extractor rather than intuition.

Running the kpex field solver correctly

One trap is worth writing down because it silently produces confident wrong numbers. kpex --mesh does nothing. kpex invokes FasterCap in auto mode, FasterCap -b -i -v -a0.05 -d0.5 -m0.5 -f2 -ap, and FasterCap's own log says "Auto calculation with max error: 0.05. Remark: Auto option overrides all other Manual settings". A four-point --mesh sweep returns four identical numbers, which reads as a converged result and is nothing of the kind.

The knobs that work are --delaunay_amax, the KLayout-side triangulation, default 50 um², and --tolerance, FasterCap's auto target. On a 0.2 um gap the default triangulation is worth 11 % on the coupling.

Judge convergence from the raw capacitance matrix's asymmetry, weighted by magnitude and thresholded at 0.1 fF so a sign flip on a numerically zero entry does not dominate. Below about 0.2 % the answer has settled. The 18.7 % on capsub_tm2 is why those rows in table D are greyed out rather than quoted. The triangulation is rebuilt from scratch each run, so neighbouring points carry uncorrelated scatter, and that scatter is the error bar.

Two further things to know before comparing a field solve to a model. kpex reports the far-field residual, the flux that escapes the simulation box, as Cext_i_i tied to VSUBS in the emitted netlist. It is a boundary artifact, between 0.17 and 4.2 fF on this bench, and it belongs in its own column, not added to the substrate term. And on a layout containing devices the totals are not comparable at all: Magic deliberately excludes the device region because the compact models cover it, while FasterCap treats the gate and the diffusion as plain conductors. Only device-less structures give an apples-to-apples capacitance comparison, which is why the whole bench is metal-only.

On resistance, kpex drives Magic rather than solving anything itself, using a different recipe than sak-pex.sh does: ext2sim plus extresist all with the deprecated extresist tolerance, against sak-pex's extract do resistance plus the threshold/mindelay/minres triple. On the template inverter that recipe gives 881 resistors and, notably, the correct total capacitance of 288.94 fF, so it avoids defects 1 and 4. Its resistor topology on the device-less bench cases is not right (on the asymmetric tee it emits the correct star plus a duplicate degenerate mesh), but kpex's resistance support is young and device-less nets are outside what extresist is built for, so that is recorded as an observation rather than counted as a defect.

What to do

  1. Do not ship a full-RC netlist from this flow yet. Neither setting of EXT_MODE=3 is usable: MINDELAY=1 gives correct capacitance with a wrong resistor network, MINDELAY=0 gives a correct resistor network with doubled coupling. Use EXT_MODE=2 for capacitance and treat resistance separately until this is fixed.

  2. Drop ext2spice lvs from sak-pex.sh and set the options it was wanted for explicitly, leaving hierarchy off. That is a one-line change and it recovers 16 % of the substrate capacitance, and it makes the two PEX targets agree.

  3. Stop using EXT_MODE=1. It is faster because it extracts less, and what it leaves out is the interconnect coupling. Correct the word "decoupled" in both macro READMEs and in the sak-pex.sh header to say the coupling is omitted.

  4. Add a mode-2 against mode-3 total-capacitance check to the flow. The two modes run on the same geometry with the same capacitance deck, so their per-net totals should agree within the RC node split. A ratio near 2 is the double count, and it is a few lines against netlists the flow already produces.

  5. Report defects 1, 2, 5 and 6 upstream to Magic with the cross, tee, sidewall and overlap cases. They are four shapes each, they reproduce in under a second, and defect 5 comes with kpex's 2.5D engine as an independent second reading of the same PDK file.

  6. Ask IHP to re-fit the sidewall coefficients against a field solve. Even with the factor of two corrected, the 1/(s+off) form is 61 % high at 0.2 um spacing and 12 % low at 1.6 um. The five converged FasterCap points in table F are enough to start from.

  7. Keep the bench. Every defect here reproduces in seconds on a handful of rectangles, and the same 31 layouts will tell you immediately whether an upstream fix worked.
